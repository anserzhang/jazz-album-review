Famous jazz musicians tend to be very busy people. Between tours, recording sessions and educational endeavors, they don’t always have surplus time to, in the parlance of our times, “create content.” Emails and calls and texts can go straight into the ether.

著名的爵士音乐家往往是极其忙碌的人。在巡演、录音与教学事务之间，他们并不总能像我们这个时代所说的那样去“产出内容”。邮件、电话和短信常常就那样消失在虚空中。

And yet, when we began assembling this tribute to John Coltrane, on the occasion of his centennial on September 23, the replies often came back in short order and over word count. It seemed as though even the most in-demand improvising musicians just had to pay homage. Their lifelong adoration of this music was simply too compelling. Our tribute to Miles Davis roused a similar enthusiasm, but the fervor of Coltrane’s performances gives these testimonials a different air of awakening and genuflection. Before his untimely passing in 1967, at age 40, Coltrane famously said he wanted to be a saint—likely in jest, given the modesty at his core. Nevertheless, the spiritual might of his work is undeniable.

然而，当我们开始编纂这部向约翰·柯川致敬的作品时——恰逢9月23日他的百年诞辰——回复往往很快就来了，而且字数超出所求。看起来，那些最炙手可热的即兴演奏家也非得前来致敬不可，他们对这份音乐毕生的热爱实在难以抑制。我们此前对迈尔斯·戴维斯的致敬同样唤起了类似的热情，但柯川演奏所具有的那种炽热，让这些证言带上了另一种气息——觉醒与跪拜。在1967年英年早逝、年仅40岁之前，柯川曾说自己想成为一位圣徒——考虑到他骨子里的谦逊，这多半是一句玩笑话。尽管如此，他作品中的精神力量是不容否认的。

Coltrane is arguably the most influential saxophonist in jazz history, as well as one of the great artists in any sphere. Relentlessly self-improving, he was a virtuoso who reshaped the jazz language in his own image, opening up new harmonic pathways in the post-bop era and redefining standards of technique and pure power. At the helm of his Classic Quartet, he architected a notion of long-form improvisation as a collective quest for enlightenment, and that concept would continue into the fearlessly avant-garde settings he pursued in his final years. He elevated the soprano saxophone; he used extended techniques to broaden the expressive capabilities of his instrument; he led the charge at Impulse!, turning the label into an iconic jazz brand. Perhaps most important, he conquered addiction and replaced it with a searching passion for life.

柯川可以说是爵士史上最具影响力的萨克斯手，也是任何领域里最伟大的艺术家之一。他永不停歇地自我提升，是一位以自己的形象重塑了爵士语言的演奏大师——在后波普时代开辟出新的和声路径，也重新定义了技巧与纯粹力量的标准。在他率领经典四重奏的岁月里，他构建出一种观念：长形式即兴演奏是一场集体式的求索启蒙；而这一理念延续到了他生命最后几年所追求的无畏前卫之中。他提升了高音萨克斯的地位；他用扩展技巧拓宽了乐器的表现力；他在 Impulse! 领衔冲锋，把这家厂牌变成了爵士乐的标志性品牌。或许最重要的是，他战胜了毒瘾，用一种对生命的探索式激情取代了它。

The renowned musicians who curated this project chose tracks that run the gamut of Coltrane’s oeuvre—from those free-jazz breakthroughs to modal explorations, hard bop and more. Through all of it, he serves as a lodestar for any artist who understands how studying, progressing and living with intention can have a profound emotional and spiritual impact on the work. To Trane, the music, the humanity and the celestial were one.

策划这个项目的知名音乐家们各自挑选了覆盖柯川全部创作光谱的曲目——从自由爵士的突破之作、模态探索、硬波普，一直到更多风格。在这一切之中，对于每一位懂得如何以研习、进步与有意识的方式生活、并明白这会对作品产生深刻情感与精神影响的艺术家来说，他都是一枚指路明灯。对柯川而言，音乐、人性与天界，本就是一体的。

“My Little Brown Book” - Duke Ellington & John Coltrane (Impulse!, 1963)《My Little Brown Book》—— 杜克·埃灵顿与约翰·柯川（Impulse!, 1963）

Duke Ellington & John ColtraneDuke Ellington

As modern jazz musicians, we are often enamored with complexity and disdainful of simplicity. One of the masters of complexity is John Coltrane—until he’s not. Coltrane’s harmonically complicated music is the stuff of legend. What is often overlooked is his capacity for beauty, and for simplicity when it’s called for. This is beautiful. – Branford Marsalis

作为当代爵士乐手，我们常常迷恋复杂、轻视简单。约翰·柯川是复杂主义的大师之一——直到他忽然不复杂了。柯川那套和声繁复的音乐早已是传奇。经常被忽略的是，他同样有对美的追求，也有在需要时做到简单的能力。这一段就是美的。—— 布兰福德·马萨利斯（Branford Marsalis）

“Speak Low” - Sonny Clark, Sonny’s Crib (Blue Note, 1958)《Speak Low》—— 桑尼·克拉克《Sonny's Crib》（Blue Note, 1958）

Sonny's CribSonny Clark

Back in high school, when I was first discovering this music, I didn’t have a whole lot of money to spend on records. I would buy these compilations. There was some great stuff. Thelonious Monk and Sonny Rollins on Riverside. The Best of Ornette Coleman and The Best of John Coltrane on Atlantic. One of the first, if not the first, “jazz” records I bought was Blue Note’s Three Decades of Jazz, Volume 1 - 1949–1959. On this double album were Monk, Rollins, Clifford Brown, Miles Davis, Art Blakey, Jimmy Smith, Milt Jackson, Bud Powell, Horace Silver, J.J. Johnson, Sonny Clark, Lou Donaldson and John Coltrane.

高中时我刚开始迷上这音乐，手头没多少钱买唱片，就买各种合集，里面有不少好东西：Riverside 的塞洛尼斯·蒙克与桑尼·罗林斯，Atlantic 的《The Best of Ornette Coleman》和《The Best of John Coltrane》。我买的第一张（如果不是第一张，就是最早几张之一）“爵士”唱片，是 Blue Note 的《Three Decades of Jazz, Volume 1 - 1949–1959》。这张双张专辑里有蒙克、罗林斯、克利福德·布朗、迈尔斯·戴维斯、阿特·布雷基、吉米·史密斯、米尔特·杰克逊、巴德·鲍威尔、霍勒斯·西弗、J.J. 约翰逊、桑尼·克拉克、卢·唐纳森和约翰·柯川。

The track I’ve chosen is from that collection. Coltrane is not the leader. The song is “Speak Low,” from Sonny Clark’s album Sonny’s Crib, with Donald Byrd, Curtis Fuller, Paul Chambers, Art Taylor and Coltrane. I believe these were the very first notes I heard of John Coltrane. That sound! The way he sang that melody! How do you describe that? No words. I was stunned. I’m still stunned. – Bill Frisell

我选的这首就在那张合集里。领奏的不是柯川——是《Speak Low》，出自桑尼·克拉克的专辑《Sonny's Crib》，与他同台的是唐纳德·伯德、柯蒂斯·福勒、保罗·钱伯斯、阿特·泰勒和柯川。我想，我最早听到的柯川的音符就是这些。那个音色！他唱那条旋律的方式！这该怎么形容？没词了。我被击中了，至今依然。—— 比尔·弗里塞尔（Bill Frisell）

“Your Lady” - Live at Birdland (Impulse!, 1964)《Your Lady》—— 《Live at Birdland》（Impulse!, 1964）

Live At BirdlandJohn Coltrane

I’ve always loved this tune, recorded at the same session that produced the masterpiece “Alabama”; it’s so incredibly swinging.

我一直喜欢这首曲子，它录自成就了杰作《Alabama》的同一次录音，那次录音的律动强得不可思议。

It’s one of my favorite performances of Coltrane on soprano. His exploratory and bluesy sides are in total balance, and Elvin Jones plays with ferocious forward motion and relaxed intensity. It’s a deep expression of joy. – Chris Potter

这是我最喜欢的一版柯川的高音萨克斯演奏。他探索性的一面与布鲁斯的一面达成了完美的平衡，而埃尔文·琼斯打得既有凶猛的推进力，又保持松弛的强度。这是一种深沉的喜悦的表达。—— 克里斯·波特（Chris Potter）

“Transition” - Transition (Impulse!, 1970)《Transition》—— 《过渡》（Impulse!, 1970）

TransitionJohn Coltrane

The fire, the fluidity, the corporeal, the celestial, the churning current, the roiling wind, the coexistent centrifugal and centripetal force, the searing visceral power, the soaring devotional aspiration…

火焰、流动、肉身、天穹、翻涌的暗流、咆哮的风、离心力与向心力同时共存、灼烧般的直觉力量、直冲云霄的虔敬升腾……

A friend of mine once described listening to the John Coltrane Quartet as akin to watching the crest of an incoming wave. If so, then “Transition” is like surfing a tsunami. This is the track that singlehandedly got 18-year-old me through my first brutal Boston winter. And, to this day, some 40 years later, it’s almost impossible to imagine another 15 minutes of music that could ever generate as much heat. – Joshua Redman

我有个朋友曾形容，听约翰·柯川四重奏就像看着一道迎面而来的浪头卷起。如果这么说的话，那《Transition》就是在一道海啸上冲浪。这首曲子当年单枪匹马地把我——18岁那年——从波士顿第一个严酷的冬天里撑了过来。时至今日，四十年过去，我几乎无法想象还有哪一段15分钟的音乐能烧出这么多热量。—— 约书亚·雷德曼（Joshua Redman）

“Trinkle, Tinkle” - Thelonious Monk With John Coltrane (Jazzland, 1961)《Trinkle, Tinkle》—— 《塞洛尼斯·蒙克与约翰·柯川》（Jazzland, 1961）

Thelonious Monk With John ColtraneThelonious Monk

I have always been drawn to John Coltrane’s lesser-documented collaborations with Thelonious Monk, and to the special interaction between these two heroes of mine. I picked up this album as a teenager, and it’s been a favorite ever since. Coltrane’s interpretation of Monk’s music gives it a whole different feeling. This track in particular, recorded in 1957, has Coltrane absolutely flying. I love the use of space—how Monk makes the perfect choice to drop out during Coltrane’s solo, allowing him to take off into a vertiginous dance. – Mary Halvorson

我一直被约翰·柯川与塞洛尼斯·蒙克那些较少被记录的合作吸引，也被这两位我心中的英雄之间那种特殊的互动吸引。我十几岁时买下这张唱片，从此一直是最爱之一。柯川对蒙克音乐的诠释带来完全不同的感受。这一首录于1957年，柯川简直在飞。我喜欢他对“留白”的运用——蒙克在柯川独奏时恰好选择退出，把空间让出来，让他腾空而起，跳出一段令人眩晕的舞蹈。—— 玛丽·霍尔沃森（Mary Halvorson）

“Dear Lord” - Transition (Impulse!, 1970)《Dear Lord》—— 《过渡》（Impulse!, 1970）

One of my absolute favorite Coltrane compositions, and one I feel is played far less often than it should be, is “Dear Lord.” There’s such a human quality to it, a simplicity and familiarity that has always drawn me in. The melody feels honest and unguarded, and to me it feels like a beautiful and calm testimony—like the prayer and thanks that we give when we simply stop to acknowledge all that we have. There’s a quiet gratitude in it. Also, knowing that he and Alice were married the same year it was recorded—1965—adds another layer to it. I hear an unguarded contentment. – Brandee Younger

《Dear Lord》是我最喜欢的柯川作品之一，我也觉得它被演奏的次数远远少于它应得的。它有一种极其人性的质地——那种简单与熟悉，一直把我吸引过去。旋律显得真诚而毫无防备，在我听来就像一份美丽而平静的见证——像我们停下来承认自己拥有的一切时，向上帝献上的祈祷与感恩。它里面有一种安静的谢意。而且，知道他和艾丽丝是在录制的同一年——1965年——结婚的，又给它添了一层意味。我听出了一种不加修饰的知足。—— 布兰迪·扬格（Brandee Younger）

“Straight, No Chaser” - Miles Davis, Milestones (Columbia, 1958)《Straight, No Chaser》—— 迈尔斯·戴维斯《里程碑》（Columbia, 1958）

Milestones (Mono version)Miles Davis

The Coltrane track I choose is from the 1958 Miles Davis recording, Milestones, and the piece is “Straight, No Chaser” by Thelonious Monk. Coltrane has the third solo, and it is a masterpiece of swing, invention and spontaneity that has haunted me for the past 68 years. – John McLaughlin

我选的柯川曲目来自迈尔斯·戴维斯1958年的录音《Milestones》，曲子是塞洛尼斯·蒙克的《Straight, No Chaser》。柯川拿的是第三个solo，那是一段融合摇摆、创意与即兴的杰作，纠缠了我68年。—— 约翰·麦克劳克林（John McLaughlin）

“Body and Soul” - Coltrane’s Sound (Atlantic, 1964)《Body and Soul》—— 《柯川之声》（Atlantic, 1964）

Coltrane's SoundJohn Coltrane

I love all things Trane. But the period that really resonates with me is Trane’s period with Atlantic Records. He first began to document his experiments with the “three-tonic system” during this period, which is a kind of orbicular harmonic device in which there are three tonal centers, dividing the octave into three equal parts. He employed this technique in perhaps his best-known composition, “Giant Steps,” but he continued to use it as an improvisational device for his entire life.

关于柯川的一切我都爱。但真正与我产生共鸣的，是他在 Atlantic 唱片的那段时期。正是在那时，他开始记录自己关于“三调性体系”的实验——这是一种球形的和声装置，设有三个调性中心，把一个八度平分成三等份。他在自己可能最著名的作品《Giant Steps》中运用了这一技法，但终其一生都把它当作即兴工具。

Trane was one of the most eloquent and thoughtful composers our music has ever seen—and the Atlantic albums really shine a light on this aspect of his art. I can’t recommend all of his Atlantic albums highly enough, but if I had to choose a favorite, it would be Coltrane’s Sound, which features, among others, the compositions “Satellite,” “26-2” (on the CD reissues) and his progressive take on the old chestnut “Body and Soul.” – Jeff Parker

柯川是我们这门音乐所见过最雄辩、最深思熟虑的作曲家之一——而 Atlantic 时期的专辑真正照亮了他艺术的这一面。他的 Atlantic 专辑我怎么说推荐都不够，但如果必须挑一张最爱，那就是《Coltrane's Sound》，其中收录了《Satellite》《26-2》（在 CD 再版中），以及他对那首老掉牙的《Body and Soul》颇具前瞻性的处理。—— 杰夫·帕克（Jeff Parker）

“Crescent” - Crescent (Impulse!, 1964)《Crescent》—— 《新月》（Impulse!, 1964）

CrescentJohn Coltrane Quartet

This composition and complete album are a masterpiece. Saxophonist Supreme John Coltrane captures us with the beauty of his tone, the soulfulness of his expression and the feeling of his spirit-rhythm from the love in his heart.

这首作品、这张完整的专辑，都是杰作。至高无上的萨克斯手约翰·柯川用他音色的美、表达的深度，以及从心底之爱中生出的那种“灵性律动”，把我们牢牢抓住。

To create your song within the song is what I’ve learned through the years listening to Maestro Coltrane. “Crescent” is one of the feature compositions on my Blue Note release Joyous Encounter, featuring Hank Jones, George Mraz and Paul Motian. They provided the perfect foundation for me to try and capture a personal interpretation of the spiritual feeling within the composition’s beauty. Follow the sound and let it take you places. – Joe Lovano

这些年聆听柯川大师，让我学会在歌中造出另一首歌。《Crescent》是我那张 Blue Note 唱片《Joyous Encounter》里的主打作品之一，由汉克·琼斯、乔治·姆拉兹和保罗·莫提安与我共同完成。他们提供了完美的地基，让我得以尝试为这首作品美感中的那份精神性，做一次属于我个人的诠释。跟着声音走，让它带你去某个地方。—— 乔·洛瓦诺（Joe Lovano）

“Love” - Meditations (Impulse!, 1966)《Love》—— 《沉思录》（Impulse!, 1966）

Meditations (Originals Version)John Coltrane

There is something profoundly human in the restraint of “Love.” The melody is disarmingly simple, yet it feels inevitable ... like something already known, just waiting to be heard. Coltrane’s sound carries it with such warmth and clarity that every note feels fully present, unforced and deeply lived. In that simplicity, beauty and truth become the same thing. – Melissa Aldana

《Love》的克制里有某种极其人性的东西。旋律简单得让人卸下防备，却又让人觉得不可避免……像是某种早已知晓、只等被听见的东西。柯川的音色以那样的温暖与清晰托住它，每一个音符都显得完整在场、不事勉强、带着被真正活过的深度。就在那份简单里，美与真变成了同一件事。—— 梅丽莎·阿尔达纳（Melissa Aldana）

“Equinox” - Coltrane’s Sound (Atlantic, 1964)《Equinox》—— 《柯川之声》（Atlantic, 1964）

Here’s a recent memory: When we lost Pharoah coming up on four years, it was just after the equinox and it hit me hard. Raymond Pettibon took me to see Pharoah up in Hollywood like five different times, so the connect was in-person experiences. The only one we saw more was Elvin … the closest I ever got to John Coltrane with live music was hearing the cats he played with, so it was profound for me.

Then track six came on, and everything changed. “Naima.” This wasn’t about speed or complexity. It was about sound. It was about feeling. There were so many things that captivated me. I didn’t understand the details, but I knew exactly how it made me feel. That day, I realized my point of entry to this music wouldn’t be through my head first, but through my heart.

我吹了几年萨克斯之后，所有人开始跟我说同一句话：“你要是想吹到高水平，就必须学会《Giant Steps》。”于是我买了那张专辑，拿着我的随身听坐下，满怀期待地想听听大家都在说什么。我被淹没了。我能感受到那技艺的分量，但它远远超出我能理解或想象的演奏水平。然后第六轨响起，一切都变了。《Naima》。这不是关于速度或复杂度，而是关于声音，关于感受。有太多东西吸引了我。我不理解其中的细节，但我确切地知道自己被带到了什么状态。那天我意识到：进入这门音乐的入口，对我来说不会先经由大脑，而是先经由心脏。

Seven years later, I was getting ready to leave for Berklee College of Music. By then, I could semi-successfully play chorus after chorus on “Giant Steps.” I practiced Coltrane’s solo along with the record almost daily and played the tune at gigs and sessions whenever I could. On a flight to a music camp, I put the album on and played it from the beginning. It had been years since I’d listened all the way through, as it was usually “Giant Steps” on repeat. Then track six came on. “Naima” came on.

七年后，我正准备去伯克利音乐学院报到。那时我已经能勉强一段接一段地吹《Giant Steps》的 chorus。我几乎每天对着录音练习柯川的独白，只要有机会就在演出和录音棚里演这首曲子。在飞去一个音乐营地的航班上，我把这张专辑放出来，从头开始吹。距离我上一次完整听完全程已经过了很多年，因为通常我只循环播放《Giant Steps》那一轨。然后第六轨响了。《Naima》响了。

It felt like I was hearing it for the first time all over again. Somewhere along the way, I’d gotten lost. I had become consumed by technique—by speed, virtuosity and complexity. This time, I understood so much more of what was happening with the harmony, the form and the architecture of the piece, but I was also reminded of exactly what had captivated me at 10 years old. The sound. The feeling. In that moment, I realized my pursuit of mastering “Giant Steps” had to pause, and from then on, I wanted to focus my energy on emotion, melody and sound.

感觉就像再一次重新听到它。某个时刻，我走偏了。我被技巧吞没——被速度、炫技和复杂度。这一次，我对和声、曲式与作品结构正在发生的事理解了多得多的东西，但我也被提醒起，10岁那一年真正吸引我的究竟是什么：声音，感受。在那一刻我意识到，对《Giant Steps》的精通之路必须先暂停，从此我要把精力放在情感、旋律与声音上。

After 12 or so years of traveling the world, performing and recording with heroes and peers, I finally felt equipped to make an album that truly represented what I love most about music. When I sat down to write the music, I kept returning to the same place I’d discovered as a 10-year-old saxophonist. Every piece began with one question: “What if it started from the place that ‘Naima’ comes from?” “Naima” wasn’t just the inspiration for one piece of music or album; it came to represent the starting point for everything I want my music to be. – Walter Smith III

在世界各地巡演了十二年左右、与那些英雄和同侪一起录音演出之后，我终于觉得自己有足够的准备，去做一张真正代表我最爱音乐的那部分东西的专辑。当我坐下来写这些曲子时，我一次次回到同一个地方——那个我10岁做萨克斯手时发现的地方。每一首作品都从同一个问题开始：“如果它从《Naima》所来自的那个地方开始呢？”《Naima》不再只是某一首曲子或某一张专辑的灵感来源，它成了我想要的一切音乐的起点。—— 沃尔特·史密斯三世（Walter Smith III）

“Expression” - Expression (Impulse!, 1967)《Expression》—— 《表达》（Impulse!, 1967）

ExpressionJohn Coltrane

As a 13-year-old beginning saxophonist in 1967, I had access to my trumpeter stepfather’s record collection. It was not long after Coltrane’s passing, and this album was a recent addition to his collection. He sat me down and had me listen to the title track. He explained to me that we were listening to a man who was probably in deep pain, who was reaching for the ineffable. This impassioned, dynamic recording is the mature work of a master musician very near the end of his shortened life.

1967年，我13岁，刚起步吹萨克斯，能接触到继父——一位小号手——的唱片收藏。那是柯川去世后不久，这张专辑是他收藏里新添的一张。他让我坐下，听那段标题曲，并向我解释：我们听到的是一个大概正深陷痛苦的人，一个正在伸手够向那不可言说之物的人。这段激情饱满、充满动势的录音，是一位大师在生命被缩短的尽头所做出的成熟之作。

It’s not one of Trane’s “hits,” and it wasn’t played by his most well-known quartet. But it is an example of musicianship—rarely achieved—that reflects deep listening and empathy within a group that included his wife, pianist Alice Coltrane, and the young, spiritually energetic drummer Rashied Ali. I can only imagine what it must have felt like to be at that session.

它不是柯川的“热门曲”，也不是由他最著名的四重奏演奏的。但它是一份极少能达到的音乐家水准的样本——一个包含他妻子、钢琴手艾丽丝·柯川，以及年轻、充满精神能量的鼓手拉希德·阿里的组合，展现出深刻的倾听与共情。我只能想象身处那场录音现场会是什么感觉。

This music shines in the void of human anguish, against the oblivion of eternal darkness. As such, it is one of my guiding lights. – Lenny Pickett (Musical Director, Saturday Night Live; solo artist)

这音乐在人类苦痛的虚空中发光，抵挡着永恒黑暗的湮没。因此，它是我的一盏指路灯。—— 伦尼·皮克特（Lenny Pickett）

“Blues Minor” - Africa/Brass (Impulse!, 1961)《Blues Minor》—— 《非洲／铜管》（Impulse!, 1961）

Africa/BrassJohn Coltrane Quartet

John Coltrane was a major spiritual and cultural figure when I was growing up in Berkeley, California. I remember teachers talking about him in high school—not music teachers, but English teachers and history teachers. He left a huge impression on the Bay Area in the late ’60s and ’70s. It seemed like all the music we were hearing had his DNA. I started listening to Africa/Brass later on in life, in my 40s, so it’s in a fresher mental file for sure. “Blues Minor” reminds me of the Coltrane we would hear on the radio: swinging, swinging, swinging, with the pure cry and intensity of his saxophone. – Steven Bernstein

我在加州伯克利长大的时候，约翰·柯川是一位重要的精神与文化人物。我记得高中时老师们谈起他——不是音乐老师，而是英语老师和历史老师。他在60年代末到70年代给湾区留下了巨大的印象。我们当时听到的音乐，好像都带着他的 DNA。我是在人生更晚的时候、40多岁那年才开始听《Africa/Brass》的，所以它在我脑中的档案确实更新鲜。《Blues Minor》让我想起当年收音机里播的那个柯川：摇摆、摇摆、摇摆，带着他萨克斯那种纯粹的呐喊与强度。—— 史蒂文·伯恩斯坦（Steven Bernstein）

“All or Nothing at All” - Ballads (Impulse!, 1963)《All or Nothing at All》—— 《抒情集》（Impulse!, 1963）

What I love about this piece is how Coltrane explores every way to approach the melody. He plays it clearly but takes many beautiful melodic liberties, making it both a solo and a melodic statement in one. You experience everything in a chorus and a half. It exemplifies Coltrane’s brilliance, capturing a range of expression without losing focus, and offering a sonic illustration.

我爱这首曲子的地方在于，柯川探索了通向旋律的每一条路径。他把它弹得清晰，却又大举放开旋律的边界，让它同时成为一段solo和一次旋律陈述。你在半个 chorus 之内就经历了一切。它展现了柯川的才华：捕捉极广的表达范围而不失焦点，并提供了一份声音的图解。

Coltrane tells a story with the melody. As the piece evolves, he opens up and takes you on a journey. His approach is truly “all or nothing.” The tension and release created by the rhythm section and Coltrane’s improvisations generate excitement and drive. The layered melodic sections are perfectly paced, guiding the listener through a broad emotional arc in, again, just a chorus and a half. You can feel the song’s meaning and the story’s weight in how he and the band perform it. Notably, there is never a traditional melody-solo-melody structure. This approach is ingenious, with Coltrane proving that he too is the story—how he plays and paces himself, supported by a responsive rhythm section that shares the message. It’s honest, intentional, expressive and captures life’s tensions, joys and triumphs—a sonic sermon.

柯川用旋律讲故事。随着曲子展开，他打开局面，带你踏上一段旅程。他的处理方式确实是“孤注一掷”。节奏组与柯川的即兴所制造的紧张与释放，产生兴奋感与推动力。层层叠加的旋律段落节奏安排得恰到好处，引导听者穿过一条宽阔的情感弧线——而且，还是在仅仅半个 chorus 之内。你能从他与乐队的演奏方式中感受到这首歌的意义与故事的重量。值得注意的是，这里从来没有传统的主歌—独奏—主歌结构。这个处理极为巧妙：柯川证明了他本身就是那个故事——他的弹法、他掌控节奏的方式，脚下有一个懂得回应、与他共享同一信息的节奏组支撑。它诚实、有意图、有表达，捕捉了生命的张力、喜悦与凯旋——一篇声音的布道词。

In my view, this is the best version of this classic song. Coltrane made it his own, showing that it’s about more than just playing a standard or an original. It’s about what the player brings to the song and how the rhythm section upholds the vision. This performance inspired me to see music as a vehicle for conveying a song’s message and meaning while leaving the listener wanting more. – Camille Thurman

在我看来，这是这首经典歌曲最好的版本。柯川把它变成了自己的东西，表明它不只是“演一首标准曲”或“一首原创”那么简单。它关乎演奏者带进歌里的东西，以及节奏组如何支撑起那个构想。这一版演奏让我看到：音乐可以是一辆承载歌曲信息与意义的载体，同时让听者意犹未尽。—— 卡米尔·瑟曼（Camille Thurman）

“My Favorite Things” - My Favorite Things (Atlantic, 1961)《My Favorite Things》—— 《我最喜欢的东西》（Atlantic, 1961）

My Favorite ThingsJohn Coltrane

I’d have to agree with McCoy Tyner and Miles Davis, who told me, “You and John feel the same way about the music.” It’s with love and compassion that we play this healing music for all the people to hear. Turn the Music Up—my new record—does exactly that! – Azar Lawrence

麦考伊·泰纳和迈尔斯·戴维斯曾对我说：“你和约翰对音乐的感觉是一样的。”我不得不认同他们。我们怀着爱与怜悯演奏这种疗愈的音乐，让所有人都能听到。《Turn the Music Up》——我的新专辑——做的正是这件事！—— 阿扎·劳伦斯（Azar Lawrence）

“Ogunde” - The Olatunji Concert: The Last Live Recording (Impulse!, 2001)《Ogunde》—— 《奥拉通吉音乐会：最后的现场录音》（Impulse!, 2001）

The Olatunji Concert: The Last Live Recording (Live)John Coltrane

I have great admiration for what culminated in Coltrane’s final documented live work after a lifetime pursuit of mastery and creative excellence. I remember hearing this for the first time as a young person and being completely transported by the screams and howls of the saxophones, which blur the line between pleasure and pain. The lo-fi distorted quality of the 1967 recording only adds to the immediacy of a performance focused on setting aside earthly attachments in favor of complete immersion in the moment. I am grateful to Coltrane for the creative model he left us, as it shows what is possible when potential is pursued to its fullest through a lifetime of devotion. – Anthony Pirog (The Messthetics; Janel and Anthony; solo artist)

在一生对精进技艺与创作卓越的追求之后，最终凝结成柯川有据可查的最后一次现场录音，我对此怀有极大的敬意。我记得年轻时第一次听到它，被萨克斯的尖叫与嚎叫彻底带走——那些声音模糊了愉悦与痛苦之间的界线。1967年录音的低保真失真质感，只为这场演出增添了更多临场感：它专注于把尘世的牵绊放在一边，换取对当下的完全沉浸。我感谢柯川留给我们的创作范本，因为它展示出：当潜能被一生的投入推到极致时，能抵达什么位置。—— 安东尼·皮罗格（Anthony Pirog）

Appendix · Original Liner Notes — Duke Ellington & John Coltrane (Impulse!, 1963)附录 · 唱片原始内页说明 —— 《Duke Ellington & John Coltrane》（Impulse!, 1963）

以下为该唱片原版内页说明的原文照录与对照翻译（内页说明署名 Stanley Dance）。原文首词排版缺字（“he classic” 应为 “The classic”），译文按完整词处理，仅调整段落划分。

The classic 1962 album Duke Ellington & John Coltrane showcased the rising jazz saxophone innovator performing alongside the long-established piano institution. While the pairing might have portended a dynamic clash of the musical generations, instead we got a casual, respectful, and musically generous meeting of like-minded souls.

这张经典的 1962 年专辑《Duke Ellington & John Coltrane》，让正当红的爵士萨克斯创新者，与那位早已坐镇的钢琴泰斗同台。怎么看都像是要来一场代际碰撞式的硬仗，结果我们等来的却是一场轻松、彼此尊重、且在音乐上慷慨大气的知音相会。

Similarly, while one might have assumed that Ellington would use his sidemen, instead producer Bob Thiele (who also produced similar albums for Ellington including pairings with Louis Armstrong and Coleman Hawkins) chose to bring in Coltrane's own outfit for the proceedings. Consequently, the duo is backed here at various times by bassist Jimmy Garrison and drummer Elvin Jones, as well as alternates bassist Aaron Bell and drummer Sam Woodyard.

同样地，人们本可以料想埃灵顿会带上自己的班底，结果制作人鲍勃·蒂尔（他也为埃灵顿制作过同类企划，包括与路易斯·阿姆斯特朗、科尔曼·霍普金斯的合作）选择把柯川自己的乐队请了进来。于是这张唱片里为两人伴奏的，有时是贝斯手吉米·加里森与鼓手埃尔文·琼斯，也有替补的贝斯手阿伦·贝尔与鼓手萨姆·伍德亚德。

The most surprising aspect of the Ellington/Coltrane date is how well suited Coltrane and his group are at playing what largely ends up being Ellington's own material. While he was certainly in the nascency of his more avant-garde period in 1962, Coltrane had a deep understanding of traditional jazz vocabulary, having played in a swing band in the Navy in the 1940s and studied the style of artists like Hawkins and Ben Webster while coming up in Philadelphia.

埃灵顿／柯川这次合作最出人意料之处，在于柯川与他的乐队竟然如此适配最终大多属于埃灵顿本人的曲目。1962 年的柯川的确正处在他更为前卫阶段的萌芽期，但他对传统爵士语汇有着极深的理解——40 年代他在海军的大乐队里演奏过，在费城成长时又研究过霍普金斯、本·韦伯斯特这类乐手的风格。

Similarly, though an icon of the big-band era by the 1960s, Ellington had been on the upswing of a career resurgence ever since his dynamic performance at the 1956 Newport Jazz Festival, later released as Ellington at Newport. His meeting with Coltrane was emblematic of his renewed creativity and was one of several albums he recorded in his latter life with theretofore unexpected artists, not the least of which his other 1962 date, Money Jungle with bassist Charles Mingus and drummer Max Roach. Here, Ellington and Coltrane play a handful of well-known Ellington book numbers, including a supremely lyrical “In a Sentimental Mood” and a soulful, half-lidded version of Billy Strayhorn’s “My Little Brown Book.” Ellington even supplied the brisk original “Take the Coltrane,” allowing plenty of room for Coltrane to let loose with knotty, angular lines.

同样地，埃灵顿虽然到 60 年代早已是大乐队时代的标志性人物，但自从 1956 年纽波特爵士音乐节上那场充满动势的演出（后来以《Ellington at Newport》发行）之后，他的职业生涯就一直在回升的轨道上。他与柯川的这次相遇是他重振创作力的典型例证；也是他晚年间与一批“此前意想不到的艺术家”合作的若干专辑之一，其中最不该被忽略的，就是他同年的另一张《Money Jungle》——与贝斯手查尔斯·明格斯、鼓手马克斯·罗奇合作。在这里，埃灵顿与柯川演奏了若干首“埃灵顿曲库”里的成名作，包括一首极其抒情的《In a Sentimental Mood》，以及比利·斯特雷霍恩的《My Little Brown Book》那个醇厚、半阖着眼的版本。埃灵顿甚至还献上了一首自己的轻快作品《Take the Coltrane》，给柯川留足了放开手脚的空间，让他去写那些纠结、棱角分明的乐句。
